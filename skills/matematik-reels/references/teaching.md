# Teaching and attention rules

The goal is a video a 13-year-old finishes and remembers. Those two pull in the same direction more
often than people expect: guessing before an answer, facing a misconception and recalling afterward
all hold attention and also help learning.

## Shape (30-50 seconds, one idea)

| Time | Part | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3s | Hook | Open a question or show a tempting mistake, spoken and on screen |
| 3-8s | Guess | Make the viewer commit: "Sence hangisi?" |
| 8-30s | Teach | One idea, one concrete example, one step on screen at a time |
| 30-38s | Payoff | Answer the hook's question explicitly |
| 38-45s | Recall | A new similar question, answer in the comments or next video |

If a recording departs from this, work with what was said. Don't push the teacher to reshoot unless
the opening truly loses the viewer, and then suggest one new first line.

## Hooks

Attack the mistake, never the student. Build curiosity and "ben de yapabilirim", never fear. LGS
stress is already high, and the audience is children.

Good: "Bu soruda çoğu öğrenci aynı yere bakıyor, ama cevap orada değil." / "10 saniyen var: 3/4 mü
büyük, 5/7 mi?"

Not acceptable: "Bunu bilmiyorsan LGS'yi unut." / "Bunu bilmeyen aptal." / "Son şans!"

The hook is a promise, so the video must pay it off. Invented statistics ("öğrencilerin %90'ı") are
out unless the teacher has the number.

## Screen rules

- One new thing on screen at a time, because reading and listening compete.
- Show formulas; don't caption what the formula already says.
- Before a hard step, leave a brief beat. Introduce a term by example first, then name it.
- Use red only for mistakes and green only for correct results, so color carries meaning.
- Everyday examples: harçlık, maç, oyun, kargo, market.

## Safety, since the audience is minors

These override the generic Instagram skills, which were written for adult business audiences:

- No DM calls and no keyword-in-DM funnels. Interaction stays public: comment or save. Do not use the
  `ig-dm` flow with students.
- No student names, faces, classroom footage or real student work. Rewrite mistakes as new examples.
- Do not copy LGS or textbook questions verbatim. Write a new question with the same logic.
- 5th and 6th grade content is mostly under Instagram's age limit, so its home is YouTube, marked
  "made for kids" there. Tell the teacher when the setting applies.
